About Urban Flame

A small grill house on Ember Lane, run by the two people who cook in it.

How it started

Urban Flame began as a weekend grill on a terrace in Cooke Town. Maya cooked, Rohan washed up, and the guest list was whoever answered the message. When the terrace could not hold the crowd we found a narrow room on Ember Lane with a chimney already cut through the roof, which felt like a decision being made for us.

We opened in November 2018 with fourteen dishes and one grill. The grill is still the same one. The menu has grown to thirty-three, which is as far as we intend to take it.

What we actually do

Everything savoury on the menu meets fire at some point — charcoal for the grills, a tandoor for the breads, a low flame for the dal that sits from morning until it is gone. There is no induction hob in the building and no microwave, which sounds like a boast until you realise it just means we cannot reheat anything.

Vegetables and fruit come from Russell Market most mornings. Fish arrives from Mangaluru twice a week and appears on the board only on those days. Meat is from a single butcher in Frazer Town who has been supplying us since the terrace. If a delivery does not turn up, the dish comes off the board rather than off a freezer shelf.

The people

Maya Iyer runs the kitchen and writes the menu. Rohan D’Souza runs the floor, the bookings and the accounts, and still does most of the washing up on a Sunday. Between them there are six people on the team: two on the grill, one on breads, two on the floor and one who fixes whatever broke that week.

Nobody here works a double shift. The grill and the floor change over in the afternoon instead, which is why the person grilling your chicken at 22:00 started at 17:00, not at dawn.

  • Short menu

    Thirty-three dishes, tasted daily. We would rather cook a small list properly than a long one approximately.

  • No hidden charges

    Menu prices include taxes. Service is not added to the bill and tips are split evenly across the team.

  • Waste we can see

    Trim goes into stock, stock goes into the dal, and the charcoal ash goes to a nursery two streets away.
